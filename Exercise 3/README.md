Exercise 3 — TV Energy Consumption Data Story
Data Story

1, The Audience: The audience that I felt impactful for this data is individuals who fall in "The Responsible and Wise Buyers". These individuals don't just walk into an electronics store to purchase a fancy premium model TV like others might. They intend to make a responsible purchase on a TV that would last close to a decade. These individuals do question TV prices to know if they are genuinely worth the price tag.


2, Addressing the "elephant" in the room: There's a common assumption among a vast majority of people that consider newer TV's that arrive with advanced/premium display technology are more energy efficient compared to older models like LCD's. The main pulling factor would be the price tag and the "hype" built around these newer products including terms such as premium, advanced, best, durable and etc.However, the Responsible and Wise Buyers would stop for a moment before throwing their hard-earned money at something marketed like this. They would want to address the elephant in the room which is "Am I really getting a more efficient product compared to an older TV model or paying money for something that would cost me in the long run?"



3,  How are we presenting this for them?: The answer or perhaps solution to this problem can be addressed easily using a bar chart that shows the comparison of the average annual energy consumption across all three types of screen technologies which are LCD, LED and OLED. I chose to implement a bar chart as a solution because the it makes the comparison between the categories of these three visually appealing and understanding for a generic person without overwhelming them with raw data. My webpage opens up with a hook that introduces the common perception of people; the newer the tech, the better and efficient it is. 


4, What is the final takeaway for these group?: Individuals who are categorised in the Responsible and Wise Buyer category should walk away with a mindset that tells them to always do a research before making a purchase. This dataset proves assuming newer screen display technologies are perfect and energy efficient can be wrong. This mindset should be changed not just for big purchases only, but even small ones as the saying goes "big things have small beginnings."




About The Data:

1. Data Source

What does it contain: It has TV Brands, Models, ScreenTechnologies, ScreenSizes, Energy Consumption.

WHere this dataset came from: Given by lecturer.



2. Data Processing:

A summary about this data: I filtered this data to cater for Australian available models only, cleaning brand names, grouping Screen Technologies, and finally calculating the average energy consumption.


3. Privacy:

This data does not contain any personal information about individuals. It only contains information about the products specification only.


4. Accuracy and Limitations:

Although dataset and charts can produce results that are spectacular, real-world usage differs from what is tested in controlled environments. For example, manufacturer reported energy labels are based on their lab testing compared to an actual household environment where it can vary from a fixed labelled result.


5. Ethics:

My story could be easily misread or misinterpreted such as screen technologies are bad and should not be purchased. In reality, this story encourages everyone that makes purchases to check energy consumption of their TV Models before making a decision rather than discouraging a specific technology used on a TV.
