Does Screen Technology Change How Much a TV Costs to Run?

Today, many shoppers assume that the newer the display technology, the better its efficiency. Although this may sound believable, recent research shows that Australia's televisions don't hold up as expected. The newest technology in this dataset isn't the cheapest to run after all.

Average Annual Energy Consumption by Screen Technology

The chart below shows the mean labelled energy consumption (kWh per year) for TVs currently available on the Australian market, grouped by screen technology.

Source: Australian Government TV energy rating dataset, processed in KNIME (Group By: Screen_Tech, Aggregation: Mean of Labelled energy consumption (kWh/year)).

Understanding the Chart

Plain LCD sets average 334.5 kWh/year — The lowest of all 3 groups. LED-backlit LCDs average 455.3 kWh/year, and OLED sets average 486.2 kWh/year, roughly 45% higher than plain LCD.

This shows that the even the newest models that have great display technology is also the most efficient one However, this dataset proves it is totally opposite. The newer the technology, the higher the consumption compared to older LCD panels that posses simple technology.

What are the limitations?

OLED and Premium LED Models are striving towards larger screen size which independently creates and increases energy use. The ultimate takeaway would be for a buyer that is comparing 2 types of TV size to check the individual energy label rather than sticking to good technology alone which will cost them in the future. .

What is the takeaway?

When we are comparing Tv's, we should not assume or predict brand new display technologies is much more reliable and cheaper to run energy rating label on models.
